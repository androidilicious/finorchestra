One week through Finorchestra

Version-1 example, kept for the record. This page reconstructs the week from run openai-postcutoff-aefba080, made under the first version of the system: hand-set market weights, fixed 30% single-asset caps, a fixed duration band, a hand-written formula agent and a stance-times-novelty surprise term. Revision 2 (7 September 2026) replaced all of these with data-derived choices. The current run is openai-postcutoff-v3-3baad69b; the current worked week is the numbers deck for 31 July 2026. The mechanism shown here is unchanged.

Where the week starts, what happens at each station, where the two AI paths split, and where they finish. Every number is the one the system recorded that Friday.

Money moves at station 7 and nowhere else. Stations 0 to 4 are shared by both AI strategies. Station 5 is where the paths differ. The formula agent and the three yardsticks take their own routes and appear only at the finish for comparison.

The arithmetic behind these numbers (recomputed from the raw data, matches the log)

1. Z-score = (latest value − mean of the past 10 years) ÷ standard deviation of the past 10 years

The window ends the observation before the latest one, so the latest reading never grades itself. Monthly series have about 120 points in the window, daily ones about 2,500. Results are clipped to ±3.

Worked line: CPI year on year is 3.73%. Over the previous ten years it averaged 3.25% with a standard deviation of 2.06 points. (3.73 − 3.25) ÷ 2.06 = +0.234. Daily market series are dated 6 August because they are published with a one-day lag.

2. Theme = average of the z-scores, with a sign that says which way is "up"

growth    = mean(+0.216, −0.107, −(−0.108)) = +0.072
inflation = mean(+0.234, −0.236, +0.449)   = +0.149
policy    = mean(+0.662, −(+0.153))         = +0.254
financial = mean(−1.041, −0.474)           = −0.757

Unemployment rising is bad for growth, so its sign is flipped. A steeper curve means looser conditions, so it is subtracted from the funds rate in the policy theme.

3. Regime odds from two logistic curves, temperature 0.75

P(growth up)    = 1 / (1 + e^(−0.072 / 0.75)) = 0.524
P(inflation up) = 1 / (1 + e^(−0.149 / 0.75)) = 0.550

overheating = 0.524 × 0.550         = 0.288
goldilocks  = 0.524 × (1 − 0.550)   = 0.236
stagflation = (1 − 0.524) × 0.550   = 0.262
recession   = (1 − 0.524) × (1 − 0.550) = 0.214

Both themes sit near zero this week, so both curves sit near one half and the four odds come out nearly even. A theme of +0.75 would give 73%, +1.5 would give 88%.

4. Fed text: one number from the model, the rest arithmetic

stance        = 0.30   the model reads the masked 29 July statement and scores it from −1 (easing) to +1 (tightening); cached once per statement
stance_change = 0.30 − previous statement's stance = 0.30
novelty       = 1 − cosine similarity of word 1-gram and 2-gram TF-IDF vectors, 29 July vs 17 June = 0.059
surprise      = stance × (0.5 + novelty) = 0.30 × 0.559 = 0.168

The two statements share 94% of their wording, so novelty is tiny and the surprise term stays small. The surprise feeds only the formula agent and the panel; the language model sees stance, stance change and novelty directly.

5

The split: fit the wish list inside the bank's rules

What happens. A deterministic optimiser finds the best portfolio for the blended returns that obeys every rule: no fund above 30% (stocks 35%, cash 40%), volatility under 10%, duration between 2 and 7 years, capital budget 0.60, no more than 25% of the book traded in a week. A separate rule-checker then reports which rules the portfolio is pressed against. From here the two AI paths differ in one thing only: whether that report goes back to the model.

4

Done after two rounds. Traded 25.0% of the book, exactly the weekly limit.

Same opinion, two books. The clipped book is 60% in two bond funds jammed at their caps, one of which the model never asked for. The revised book carries the model's actual conviction, gold and the dollar, with bonds spread rather than pinned. Notice also that the revision ended with every bond at neutral, a retreat toward market weights. That pattern is the hypothesis in the scope note about where the feedback edge really comes from.

6
